A step-by-step guide to Wudu (ablution) and the structure of Salah.

Wudu (Ablution) — Step by Step

Wudu is required before Salah. The order below follows the sequence prescribed in the Quran (5:6) and authentic Sunnah — each part should generally be washed three times, and the sequence itself matters.

  1. Intention (Niyyah) — Silently intend in your heart that you are performing wudu for the sake of Allah. No specific words are required.
  2. Say Bismillah — Begin by saying "In the name of Allah."
  3. Wash both hands — Up to the wrists, three times, interlacing the fingers.
  4. Rinse the mouth — Three times.
  5. Sniff water into the nose and expel it — Three times.
  6. Wash the face — From the hairline to the chin, ear to ear, three times. Run wet fingers through the beard if you have one.
  7. Wash the arms — Right arm first, then left, up to and including the elbows, three times each.
  8. Wipe the head — With wet hands, once, moving from the front to the back and returning to the front.
  9. Wipe the ears — As part of the same motion — index fingers inside the ears, thumbs behind them.
  10. Wash the feet — Right foot first, then left, up to and including the ankles, three times each, ensuring water reaches between the toes.

Wudu is invalidated by things such as using the toilet, passing wind, deep sleep, or loss of consciousness. See the Duas page for the supplication recited upon completing wudu.

The Structure of Salah

Each of the five daily prayers consists of units called rak'ahs (2, 3, or 4 depending on the prayer). Each rak'ah generally follows this structure:

  1. Takbir al-Ihram — Raise the hands and say "Allahu Akbar" to begin the prayer.
  2. Qiyam (Standing) — Recite Surah al-Fatihah, followed by another portion of the Quran (in the first two rak'ahs).
  3. Ruku (Bowing) — Bow with hands on the knees, back straight, saying "Subhana Rabbiyal Adheem."
  4. Standing back up — Rise from bowing, saying "Sami' Allahu liman hamidah," then "Rabbana lakal hamd."
  5. Sujud (Prostration) — Prostrate with forehead, nose, hands, knees, and toes touching the ground, saying "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la," typically three times.
  6. Sitting between the two prostrations — Sit briefly, then prostrate a second time.
  7. Repeat — This completes one rak'ah; the cycle repeats for the remaining rak'ahs of that prayer.
  8. Tashahhud — Sit after the second rak'ah (and the final rak'ah) to recite the testimony of faith.
  9. Taslim — Conclude the prayer by turning the head to the right, then the left, saying "Assalamu alaikum wa rahmatullah" each time.

This is a general overview of the structure of Salah. Exact wording, number of rak'ahs per prayer, and minor details vary slightly between schools of thought (madhabs). If you're learning to pray for the first time, it's best to also learn directly from a knowledgeable person or a structured course, in addition to reference guides like this one.
